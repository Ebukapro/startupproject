# The "Claude" Angle — Name-First, Meaning Optional

Run 2026-09-17. Brief changed on the client's instruction: the name does not have
to relate to the product. It has to sound good and be available.

---

## The headline finding

**Of 121 pleasant, human-sounding names checked: 1 was available at $12.**

| | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Already registered | 97 | 80% |
| Available but Premium-priced | 23 | 19% |
| **Available at ~$12** | **1** | **0.8%** |

That is a 99.2% kill rate — by far the worst of any territory tested in this
project. Two-word compounds died at 72%. Market-vocabulary names died at 87%.
Human-sounding names died at 99%.

**Why.** This is the most contested namespace on the internet. A short, warm,
pronounceable word that sounds like a person is exactly what every domain
investor bought a decade ago and every startup has wanted since. Kuda, Monzo,
Chime, Oscar, Marcus, Emma, Cleo and Dave were all claimed years before a
bootstrapped company with a USD 200 ceiling could reach them. The instinct is
right; the inventory is gone.

**Worth knowing:** 23 of them are *available*, just priced as assets — including
several genuinely lovely ones: **Neva, Pavo, Lelia, Arela, Tamira, Ravella,
Vandela, Norvella, Winola, Danai, Malaika**. GoDaddy did not disclose prices.
Some premium `.com` names sell for $150–500, which would sit inside the USD 200
ceiling. **Running those 23 through a registrar bulk search that shows prices is
the single highest-value next step if you want a genuinely beautiful name.**

## Round two: keeping the name-feel, adding an awkward edge

If perfectly smooth names are gone, the surviving shape is a name that is *almost*
smooth — a real-sounding proper noun with one unusual cluster in it. 80 candidates
built that way:

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Registered / no exact match | 37 |
| Premium | 8 |
| **Available at ~$12** | **35** |

**44% survival, up from 0.8%.** The awkward edge is what keeps a name unclaimed.

---

## Screening the survivors

16 of the best went through linguistic, trademark, association and findability
screens. The results were harsh, and two catches are worth stating plainly.

**The `-enis` problem.** Three candidates I generated — *Ferrenis*, *Halvenis*,
*Tarvenis* — end in a near-homophone of a body part. For a brand that grows by
young people saying it out loud, that is fatal. *Halvenis* also contains "halve",
which is close to the worst possible word to put near someone's savings. All three
cut.

**The homophone-collapse family.** *Melvissa* collapses into Melissa, *Tervissa*
into Clarissa or Melissa. A listener types the real name and finds nothing. Both
cut. This is the same failure that killed the respelled words in the first run.

**Also cut:** *Vestanna* — it opens with **"vest"**, and the original brief bans
the `-vest` fragment outright because PiggyVest, Risevest, Crowdyvest and
Afrivestor already own it. It should not have been generated.

Linguistic verdicts on the 16: **2 PASS, 6 CAUTION, 8 FAIL.**
Trademark: **0 BLOCKED, 7 CONTESTED, 9 CLEAR.** Findability: **12 EASY, 2 WORKABLE, 2 HARD.**

**Correction worth recording:** the screening agent reported that a branded
`elowena.com` already exists. I checked the raw registry record myself and it does
not — `elowena.com` is available at standard price, `purchasable: true`. The DÔEN
"Elowena Skirt" and the skincare brand are real, and they do occupy page one, but
the domain is free.

**Composition:** no two share their first three letters (Ker · Kes · Cor · Kel ·
Fen · Dan · Jem · Tor · Elo · Sel). The `-enna/-anna` family is capped at three,
which is why *Hestenna* was cut despite scoring 82.

### On Elowena

It is the best-sounding name in this whole round and the only one a linguist would
call effortless. It ranks tenth purely because a fashion label and a skincare brand
already occupy its search results, and the brief caps a HARD-to-find name at 6 of
15 on findability. **If you are willing to spend on marketing rather than rely on
free search, Elowena is the one to take.** If you cannot, the cap is telling you
something real.

## Honest assessment against the previous ten

This round did what was asked and the names are usable, but **they are not as good
as Claude, Kuda or Monzo, and they cannot be.** Those names are effortless because
they are short, smooth and familiar — and every name with those properties is
owned. What survives at $12 is a name that is *nearly* effortless with one rough
edge: a doubled letter, an unusual cluster, a slightly foreign cadence.

Set against the earlier list, the trade is:

- **This round** gives you a name that sounds like a person or a place, carries no
  meaning, and lets you define it entirely. Cost: it says nothing on its own, so
  every scrap of association is bought with marketing you do not yet have.
- **The earlier round** (Dawnlot, Lanternlot, Torchlot, Candlelot) gives names that
  arrive with a built-in idea, do some of the explaining for you, and are warmer on
  a first hearing. Cost: less elegant, and more obviously constructed.

**Recommendation:** run the 23 Premium names through a price check first. If any of
*Neva*, *Lelia*, *Arela* or *Tamira* comes back under USD 200, take it — it beats
everything on both lists. If none does, choose between these two lists on whether
you would rather buy a meaning or build one.

## Also rejected, worth recording

**Norborin** — the closest real-world match is **Norbormide, a rodenticide**, and
`-orin` is a standard pharmaceutical and chemical suffix. It reads as a compound,
not as a name a person would trust with their money. Clean on trademark and
findability, and still unusable.

**Hestenna** (82) — cut only to cap the `-enna/-anna` family at three.

**Tuveska** — the opening syllable splits by accent ("Too-" vs "Tyoo-"), failing
the rule that a name must be right on first sight for a Nigerian *and* an
international speaker.
