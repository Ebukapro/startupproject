# Round 5.3 — Screen: Fenvyn / Zarvyn / Lormon

All three: .com confirmed available (~$12/yr, per brief). App Store (NG, iTunes Search API) returns 0 results for all three — no direct app-name collision on any of them. That parity means the decision rests on syllables, mishearing risk, and existing brand noise.

---

## FENVYN

1. **Syllables**: FEN-vyn — 2 syllables for both NG and US/UK speakers. Clean.
2. **Cross-language meaning**: No hits in Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Pidgin, Swahili, French, Portuguese, Arabic, Spanish, or Italian. "Fen" is a plain English word (marsh) — irrelevant here, no cross-language landmine found.
3. **First-sight pronunciation**: Mostly right, but the "-vyn" ending is genuinely ambiguous — could be read as short-i ("FEN-vin," like Kevin) or long-i ("FEN-vine"). Not a 100% lock on sight for either audience.
4. **Spelling-from-hearing**: Heard once, most likely gets typed "Fenvin." That's a miss, but "Fenvin" isn't a real name/word it collapses into (unlike the Kelvell→Kelvin cases), so the damage is a bad domain guess, not a redirect to a stranger's brand.
5. **Trust/likeability**: Reads as a plausible, pleasant invented name — closest in register to "Kuda" of the three.
6. **Collision risk**: A GoFundMe personal profile and an obscure Elder Scrolls NPC ("Fevyn Ralen") are the only noise; neither is commercial/financial. Low risk.
7. **Findability**: WORKABLE — Wikipedia entries for "Fen"/"Fenny" sit near it in search, minor dilution, nothing damaging.

**Verdict: USABLE.** Cleanest of the three but not perfect — the "-vyn" vowel ambiguity keeps it off STRONG.
**Biggest weakness**: ambiguous vowel sound in "-vyn" means pronunciation isn't guaranteed on first sight.
**App Store title**: `Fenvyn: Invest & Trade` (22 chars)
**Subtitle**: `Nigerian Stocks Made Simple` (27 chars)

## ZARVYN

1. **Syllables**: ZAR-vyn — 2 syllables, clean for both audiences.
2. **Cross-language meaning**: "Zar/Zār" (زار) is a known term across Arabic/North-East-African usage for a spirit-possession exorcism ritual. Marginal relevance in Nigeria (low Arabic-speaker overlap, not a Hausa loanword in common use) — flagging as a minor, not disqualifying, risk. No hits in Yoruba, Igbo, Pidgin, Swahili, French, Portuguese, Spanish, Italian.
3. **First-sight pronunciation**: Reads correctly on sight for both audiences — no ambiguity here.
4. **Spelling-from-hearing — SEVERE FLAG**: "Zarvyn" spoken aloud is one consonant away from "Marvin," a very common real name. A voice-note listener is highly likely to mishear or type "Marvin" or "Zarvin" instead of "Zarvyn." For a bootstrapped, word-of-mouth product, this is a direct hit against the core growth channel — people telling friends the name and the friends searching the wrong word.
5. **Trust/likeability**: Sounds fine as a name, but the Marvin-adjacency undercuts distinctiveness.
6. **Collision risk**: Real, live commercial noise — "ZARVYN LIMITED" is an active UK-registered company (Companies House), "Zarvyn Nexus Private Limited" is an active Indian company (incorporated June 2025), and "ZARVYN STORE" is an active Instagram retail brand. Multiple unrelated live businesses already use this exact word.
7. **Findability**: HARD — search results are already crowded with unrelated live companies and a store front; a new fintech app named Zarvyn has to fight through that plus the Marvin misspelling problem.

**Verdict: REJECT.** The Marvin mishearing risk directly undermines the client's stated word-of-mouth growth strategy, compounded by multiple already-active commercial users of the name.
**Biggest weakness**: heard aloud, collapses into "Marvin" — a real, common name — making word-of-mouth referral unreliable.
**App Store title**: `Zarvyn: Stock Trading NG` (24 chars)
**Subtitle**: `Invest in Nigerian Stocks` (25 chars)

## LORMON

1. **Syllables**: LOR-mon — 2 syllables, clean for both audiences.
2. **Cross-language meaning**: No vulgar/comic hits found in Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Pidgin, Swahili, French, Portuguese, Arabic, Spanish, or Italian. ("Lormont," a Bordeaux suburb, was historically spelled "Lormon" pre-Revolution — a neutral coincidence, not a landmine.)
3. **First-sight pronunciation**: Reads correctly on sight for both audiences.
4. **Spelling-from-hearing — SEVERE FLAG**: "Lormon" spoken is one letter away from "Mormon" (LOR-mon vs. MOR-mon) and close to "Norman." In a market as religiously attentive as Nigeria, a fintech brand that a listener mishears/mistypes as a reference to a specific religious denomination is a real reputational hazard — it can read as comic or as an unintended religious association, exactly the categories the client asked to screen for. This is worse than a simple wrong-name typo because it risks being noticed and joked about, not just mistyped.
5. **Trust/likeability**: The name itself is plausible and pleasant in isolation, but the Mormon-adjacency is the kind of thing that gets seized on socially once anyone points it out.
6. **Collision risk**: Only inactive/legacy hits — a California attorney surname, a historical French place-name spelling. No live commercial brand collision.
7. **Findability**: WORKABLE on raw search (little existing noise), but voice/autocomplete search for "Lormon app" risks autocorrect toward "Mormon," which is an unpredictable SEO/App-Store-search wildcard.

**Verdict: REJECT.** The religious-name collision is a severe, foreseeable risk for a money app trying to build trust and spread by word of mouth in a religiously attentive market — this is the single worst finding across all three candidates.
**Biggest weakness**: mishears/misspells into "Mormon," inviting jokes and unwanted religious association for a trust-dependent product.
**App Store title**: `Lormon: Invest in Stocks` (24 chars)
**Subtitle**: `Simple Stock Trading, NG` (24 chars)

## Ranked

1. **Fenvyn — USABLE** (only real flaw: ambiguous "-vyn" vowel on first sight)
2. **Zarvyn — REJECT** (collapses to "Marvin" by ear; multiple live commercial users of the name already)
3. **Lormon — REJECT** (collapses to "Mormon" by ear; religious-sensitivity risk is disqualifying for a trust product)

Recommendation: proceed with **Fenvyn**, or generate a fresh batch — do not advance Zarvyn or Lormon as-is.
