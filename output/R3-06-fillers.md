# Screen 6 — Filler/Human-Style Brand Candidates

Domains confirmed available (~$12/yr) for all six. Checked: WebSearch (meaning, collision, findability),
iTunes Search API (App Store collision, country=NG), own linguistic knowledge for the 10-language meaning
scan (these are invented tokens — no dictionary entries exist to look up; assessed phonetically/semantically
against known lexicons).

---

## 1. Selveska — USABLE
- **Meaning:** No hits in Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa/Pidgin/Swahili/French/Portuguese/Arabic/Spanish/Italian. Faint,
  pleasant echo of Italian/French "selve" (forests) — neutral-to-positive, not disqualifying.
- **Pronunciation:** sel-VESS-ka. Reads consistently for both NG and US/UK speakers.
- **Spelling from hearing:** Moderate risk — initial "Sel-" could be typed "Cel-"; medial consonant cluster
  gives some room for error.
- **Sounds like a real name:** Yes — Slavic-surname register, same lane as Monzo/Kuda.
- **Trademark/collision:** WebSearch surfaced "Selviska" — a live online accessories store, one letter off.
  No App Store match (resultCount:0).
- **Findability:** EASY — page one is all unrelated Slavic place names/surnames.
- **Biggest weakness:** near-identical existing brand "Selviska" (accessories e-commerce) — real confusion risk on sight and by ear.

## 2. Tuveska — USABLE
- **Meaning:** No hits across the ten languages. Loosely echoes "tu" (you, in Sp/Fr/It/Pt) — mild warmth, not a problem.
- **Pronunciation:** Weakest of the six on this axis — opening syllable can land as "Too-" or "Tyoo-"
  depending on accent, and NG vs US/UK speakers will not converge identically on first hearing.
- **Spelling from hearing:** Moderate-high risk — "Tuveska," "Tooveska," "Tuvezka" all plausible mishears.
- **Sounds like a real name:** Yes, Slavic/Eastern-European-surname register.
- **Trademark/collision:** "TUVES" — a Bolivian satellite TV brand — surfaced, but different sector/market, low risk. No App Store match.
- **Findability:** EASY.
- **Biggest weakness:** first-syllable pronunciation splits by accent (too- vs tyoo-), undercutting the "correct on first sight, both audiences" bar.

## 3. Jemvarra — USABLE
- **Meaning:** No hits across the ten languages. "Jem-" opening reads as a warm, familiar English nickname
  (Jem/Gem) — a genuine asset.
- **Pronunciation:** Clean — jem-VAR-ah reads the same for NG and US/UK speakers.
- **Spelling from hearing:** Moderate risk concentrated at the tail — the double "-rra" is likely to be
  heard/typed as single "-ra" ("Jemvara") or "-rah."
- **Sounds like a real name:** Reasonably, though the "-varra" ending is more exotic/invented-sounding than Kelvunda's.
- **Trademark/collision:** "JEMVAR" is a small French youth-sailing NGO in Toulon and its boat's name —
  obscure, different spelling, negligible commercial collision.
- **Findability:** EASY.
- **Biggest weakness:** trailing double-R is the one part of the word a listener is likely to drop or respell.

## 4. Norborin — REJECT
- **Meaning:** No hits in the ten languages, but the "-orin" ending is a well-worn pharmaceutical/chemical
  suffix. Confirmed by search: closest real match is **Norbormide**, a rodenticide (rat poison), alongside
  other -in/-orin compound names (Norwogonin, Norbudrine).
- **Pronunciation:** Fine — nor-BOR-in reads consistently for both audiences.
- **Spelling from hearing:** Reasonably phonetic, low-moderate risk on its own.
- **Sounds like a real name:** No — this is the test it fails outright. It reads as a drug/compound, not a
  person's name, exactly the trap flagged in the brief.
- **Trademark/collision:** No live trademark clash found, but the associative link to a poison compound is a
  real brand-safety problem, not just noise.
- **Findability:** EASY (page one is obscure chemistry), but findability doesn't rescue the core failure.
- **Biggest weakness:** reads as a pharmaceutical/rodenticide compound (Norbormide-adjacent), not a name anyone would trust with their money.

## 5. Kelvunda — STRONG
- **Meaning:** No hits across the ten languages; nothing vulgar, religious, or ethnically loaded. "Kel-"
  opening echoes "Kelvin," a familiar, trusted-sounding root.
- **Pronunciation:** Cleanest of the six — kel-VUN-da lands the same way for NG and US/UK speakers on first sight.
- **Spelling from hearing:** Low-moderate risk — only real snag is "vun" vs "von" in fast speech, otherwise close to phonetic.
- **Sounds like a real name:** Best of the set — warm, human, sits comfortably next to Kuda/Monzo/Chime in register.
- **Trademark/collision:** No meaningful hits (only "Kelvan," a Star Trek alien race — trivial). No App Store match.
- **Findability:** EASY, cleanest page one of the six.
- **Biggest weakness:** the only soft spot is "vun" possibly heard/typed as "von" in casual speech — minor.

## 6. Torbinda — USABLE
- **Meaning:** No hits across the ten languages; no vulgar/religious/ethnic flags.
- **Pronunciation:** Clean — tor-BIN-da is consistent for both audiences.
- **Spelling from hearing:** Moderate-high risk — the word sits one phoneme away from "Belinda," a common
  real name; a listener catching it in a noisy voice note has a real chance of writing "Belinda" or "Torbelinda."
- **Sounds like a real name:** Yes, comfortably.
- **Trademark/collision:** No meaningful clashes (Torbung, Torban, a fandom-wiki "Torbina" — all unrelated/obscure).
- **Findability:** EASY.
- **Biggest weakness:** the built-in "Belinda" echo — undermines distinctiveness and is the likeliest of the six to be misheard as an existing name.
