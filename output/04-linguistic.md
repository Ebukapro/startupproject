# Agent 4 — Linguistic and Cultural Validation

All 120 candidates from `02-longlist.md` assessed against six tests: (1) cross-language
meaning/connotation across Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Nigerian Pidgin, Swahili, French,
Portuguese, Arabic, Spanish; (2) Nigerian pronunciation (Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa speakers,
equal weight across all three); (3) international pronunciation (US/UK, equal weight
with test 2); (4) spelling-from-a-voice-note; (5) verb/user-noun; (6) support-call
credibility for a company holding people's money.

A handful of genuinely uncertain items (Basira, Quantara/qantara, Gudgeon, Thalweg,
Tourbillon, Klarity/Klarna) were checked against external sources; everything else is
scored from linguistic knowledge of the nine languages and of the source English/French/
Latin/German words involved.

Legend: **OK** = clears the test · **RISK** = real, named risk · **weak** = present but
not a strength (doesn't by itself fail a candidate) · flags column is terse, not
exhaustive.

## All FAILs (25), one line each

1. **Stonebourse** — "Bourse" is French for stock exchange; regulatory-claim risk, plus the French vowel trips both NG and Intl readers.
2. **Openbourse** — "Open bourse" reads as a direct claim to open exchange access; same French-pronunciation trap.
3. **Bellbourse** — Same bourse/exchange overclaim and mispronunciation risk as the rest of the family.
4. **Boursa** — Same bourse risk, plus echoes the Turkish city Bursa and mangles the French pronunciation.
5. **Boursel** — Same bourse/exchange risk and pronunciation trap.
6. **Ledgra** — Unnatural "dgr" cluster; a listener hearing "LEJ-ra" will likely type "Lejra," not "Ledgra."
7. **Tranchet** — "Tranche" is already routinely mispronounced; the "-et" ending invites a false French silent-t reading.
8. **Basline** — Sounds exactly like the common word "Baseline" (missing the e); word-of-mouth traffic goes to the wrong, taken spelling.
9. **Marline** — Spelled "Marline" but said "marlin" (the fish); anyone who hears it will type "Marlin."
10. **Thalweg** — The "Th" invites a wrong sound (true pronunciation drops the H); an unspellable German loanword from a voice note.
11. **Gudgeon** — Pronunciation trap, and "gudgeon" is a standing English idiom for "a person easily cheated" — toxic for a firm holding customer money.
12. **Ankora** — Collides with the real Italian word "ancora" (anchor); listeners spell it that way and find nothing.
13. **Kompas** — Collides with the common English word "Compass"; search traffic defaults to the real spelling.
14. **Ledjer** — Collides with the everyday finance word "Ledger"; fails the spelling test by construction.
15. **Tallee** — Collides with the common word "Tally"; same failure mode.
16. **Parrity** — Collides with the real word "Parity"; the doubled r is inaudible in speech.
17. **Tenur** — Reads as a typo of "Tenure" or "Tenor"; a listener types one of those instead.
18. **Steddy** — Collides with "Steady" in search, and reads as a steady-returns claim — the exact reason Agent 2 already cut "Steadyhelm."
19. **Klarity** — Directly echoes Klarna, a major global fintech brand, and collides with the real word "Clarity."
20. **Bourz** — Compounds the bourse/exchange risk with an almost unpronounceable clip that also reads like "boors."
21. **Merid** — Reads as a truncated typo of "Meridian"; the listener types the fuller word.
22. **Detent** — Near-identical in spelling to "détente" (the well-known diplomatic term); the actual meaning is obscure to everyone.
23. **Spandrel** — Obscure jargon nobody recognizes, no verb/noun, and the word-shape sits uncomfortably close to "scandal."
24. **Abutment** — Contains "butt," which reads aloud as "a-BUTT-ment" — undermines the dignity of a financial support call.
25. **Tourbillon** — French pronunciation almost no NG or Intl speaker gets right on sight, and effectively unspellable from a voice note.

## Strongest 15 on linguistic grounds alone

Selected purely on the six tests — clean across all nine languages, natural for
Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa/Pidgin *and* US/UK speakers equally, safe to spell from a voice
note, and credible in a support-call sentence. (Cross-cuts territory; domain
availability and trademark clearance are Agents 6–8's job, not scored here.)

1. **Homeport** (B) — real, warm compound; "You're through to Homeport" sounds like a trusted institution.
2. **Marketday** (B) — maximally natural for Nigerian ears, with a genuine positive resonance to the Igbo four-day market cycle.
3. **Tallyhouse** (B) — real words, a working verb ("tally up"), easy everywhere.
4. **Orbita** (A) — an actual word in Spanish/Portuguese/Russian; universally pronounceable, positive trajectory imagery.
5. **Denova** (A) — "de novo" (afresh) is a known term across law/medicine; clean, meaningful, easy.
6. **Fidara** (A) — root is *fides*, faith/trust — thematically apt for a company holding people's money.
7. **Tenura** (A) — evokes "tenure"/holding, fits the stock-ownership concept, clean in all nine languages.
8. **Tiller** (D) — real word, dual imagery (steering a course / tilling soil), warm and credible on a support call.
9. **Camber** (D) — short, real, easy, subtle positive-momentum imagery (a gentle upward curve).
10. **Lintel** (F) — real word, "the beam that holds the house up" — apt supportive metaphor, easy to say.
11. **Truetenor** (B) — plain-English compound, meaningful ("true tenor" = a true term), no pronunciation risk anywhere.
12. **Northbell** (B) — evocative (bearing + opening bell), zero cross-language risk.
13. **Rondeva** (A) — reads as "rendezvous," warm, rolls off the tongue in every accent tested.
14. **Keelstone** (B) — strong grounding imagery, phonetically simple, no spelling trap.
15. **Longtide** (B) — double meaning ("going long" + tide) lands naturally for a trading app without any regulatory overclaim.
