## Every FAIL, one line each (18)

1. **Candlebasis** — institutional jargon suffix ("basis" = cost basis/basis point), contradicts the warm-not-institutional brief.
2. **Torchbasis** — warm "torch" root wasted on the same cold jargon suffix.
3. **Dawnparity** — pure jargon-on-jargon pairing with a real "parity → parody" mishearing risk.
4. **Compasslot** — collides with Compass, a major US real-estate brokerage brand, and "lot" doubles the land reading.
5. **Compassbasis** — same Compass brand collision plus institutional "basis," doubly off-target.
6. **Anchorbasis** — a genuinely strong root ("anchor") killed by cold jargon, reads like a shipping/logistics firm.
7. **Traillot** — "trail lot" is literal US real-estate listing language.
8. **Fairparity** — redundant meaning, jargon suffix, and a real mishearing risk to "parody."
9. **Plainlot** — "plain" means flat open land; this is the single most literal real-estate phrase in the set.
10. **Levelbasis** — tautological (level≈basis), zero warmth, the most forgettable name tested.
11. **Fairtenor** — "tenor" is obscure bond-market jargon that collides confusingly with its musical meaning.
12. **Ordenalis** — reads as a pharmaceutical/generic-drug name, wrong clinical register for a consumer money app.
13. **Corvalta** — root echoes crow/raven (ill-omen bird) across Italian/Portuguese/Latin, and sounds corruption-adjacent.
14. **Cedarlot** — cedar has little Nigerian cultural resonance; reads as a Western real-estate subdivision.
15. **Willowlot** — willow is essentially unknown locally; "lot" makes it read as a foreign property listing.
16. **Aspenlot** — Aspen evokes a US luxury ski resort; the strongest real-estate misfire in the whole list.
17. **Quarrybasis** — "quarry" (mining pit) has no investing metaphor, plus institutional "basis," clunky to hear and spell.
18. **Steerlot** — "steer" + "lot" reads as "feedlot," an unintentional cattle-fattening-pen association.

## Strongest 12 on linguistic grounds

**Amberlis, Estavana, Paritana, Talvinia, Holdlot, Beaconlot, Torchlot, Candlelot, Dawnlot, Quiettally, Lanternlot, Routelot.**

Rationale: the 5 clean PASSes (Amberlis, Estavana, Paritana, Talvinia, Holdlot) carry no cross-language flags and score well on all four remaining tests; the 7 CAUTIONs added are the strongest of that tier — warm, guidance-themed "-lot" words (Beaconlot, Torchlot, Candlelot, Dawnlot, Lanternlot) whose only real defect is the generic ambiguity of "lot" itself (never a land-specific reading), plus Quiettally (good metaphor, minor spelling-seam risk) and Routelot (clean word, mild lot-drift). None of the FAIL-tier jargon suffixes ("-basis," "-parity," "-tenor") or real-estate/agricultural collisions (Compasslot, Traillot, Plainlot, Cedarlot, Willowlot, Aspenlot, Steerlot) made the cut.
