# Agent 2, Round 2 — 80 Candidates, Informed by the Round-1 Gate

Triggered by the brief's gate rule: fewer than 30 survived round 1 (2 did), so
Agent 2 re-runs with the failure analysis in hand. See
`output/03b-gate-round1-analysis.md`. This is repetition 1 of a permitted 2.

## The generative rule, changed

Round 1 generated names that *sound good*. Every one of those is registered.
The two survivors were **semantically arbitrary pairings** — words with no
natural bond in English, which no domain investor would speculatively register
because there is no resale story.

Round 2 therefore generates deliberately against natural collocation:

- **Dropped entirely:** `-stone`, `-house` (all four died), and 6–7 letter
  coinages (`torven`, `rondeva` both died — investors hold these, not just the
  4–5 letter ones the brief warned about).
- **Adopted:** qualifier + market noun (the `evenbasis` shape), and concrete
  noun + market noun (the `lanternlot` shape).
- **Market nouns used as the second element:** basis, tenor, parity, ledger,
  tally, lot, post. All sanctioned, none regulated. `bourse` excluded per
  Agent 7's ruling.

All hard constraints from the original brief still apply and were applied during
generation: no return-implying words, no regulated-status words, no `-vest` or
`invest`, no geography, pronounceable on first sight, no incumbent homophones.

**Trademark, linguistic and findability screening has NOT been run on these 80.**
They are domain-check candidates first. Whichever survive get the full Agent
4/5/6/7 treatment before anything is recommended.

## Honest note on quality

Round 1 produced prettier names. Round 1's pretty names are all owned. At a USD
200 ceiling the choice is between a name you cannot buy and a name you can, and
this list is tuned for the second. Within that constraint I have tried to keep
warmth and sayability — the beacon/lantern cluster and the plain-qualifier names
are the strongest of the set, and `Lanternlot` from round 1 remains genuinely good.

If none of round 2 produces something the product owner actually likes, the
honest recommendation is **not** a third generation round but a budget
conversation: at USD 500–2,000 the aftermarket opens up and names of the
`Keelstone` calibre become purchasable. That is a commercial decision, not a
naming one.
